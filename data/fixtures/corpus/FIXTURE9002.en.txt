(1) Digital records held by public bodies underpin accountability, historical research and the exercise of individual rights. Inconsistent retention practice across the Union undermines all three.

(2) Retention periods should be set by reference to the purpose for which a record was created, rather than by reference to the medium on which it is stored.

(3) The right of access to archived records should be balanced against the protection of personal data contained in them.

(4) Destruction of records should be documented, so that the absence of a record can be distinguished from its concealment.

Article 1

Subject matter

This Regulation lays down harmonised rules on the retention, archiving and disposal of digital records held by public bodies of the Member States.

Article 2

Scope

1. This Regulation applies to digital records created or received by a public body in the course of its official functions.

2. This Regulation does not apply to records of automated decisions, which are governed by Regulation (EU) 2024/9001.

3. This Regulation does not apply to records classified in the interests of national security.

Article 3

General retention periods

1. Records documenting a decision affecting the rights of a natural person shall be retained for ten years from the date of the decision.

2. Records of financial transactions shall be retained for seven years from the end of the financial year to which they relate.

3. Records of routine internal correspondence that document no decision shall be retained for two years.

4. Where two periods under this Article could apply to the same record, the longer period shall prevail.

Article 4

Extension of retention periods

1. A retention period shall be extended where the record is the subject of pending litigation, an ongoing audit, or a request for access that has not been resolved.

2. The extension shall run until six months after the conclusion of the proceeding that gave rise to it.

3. A public body shall record the reason for each extension and the date on which it was applied.

Article 5

Disposal

1. A record shall be disposed of within twelve months of the expiry of its retention period.

2. Disposal shall be irreversible and shall be documented in a disposal register recording the identifier of the record, the date of disposal, and the authorising officer.

3. The disposal register shall itself be retained permanently.

Article 6

Archiving

1. A record of enduring historical, legal or administrative value shall be transferred to the national archive rather than disposed of.

2. The national archive shall make transferred records available for public inspection thirty years after their creation, subject to Article 7.

Article 7

Access to archived records

1. Access to an archived record shall be refused, in whole or in part, where disclosure would reveal personal data relating to a living person and no overriding public interest in disclosure exists.

2. A refusal under paragraph 1 shall be reasoned in writing and shall indicate the parts of the record that have been withheld.

3. A person refused access may apply to the supervisory authority for review within 60 days of the refusal.

Article 8

Format and integrity

1. A public body shall retain digital records in a format that remains readable for the duration of the retention period.

2. Where a format is migrated, the public body shall verify and document that the content and structure of the record are preserved.

3. A public body shall apply measures sufficient to detect unauthorised alteration of a retained record.

Article 9

Penalties

1. Member States shall lay down rules on penalties applicable to infringements of this Regulation.

2. Penalties for the premature destruction of a record documenting a decision affecting the rights of a natural person shall be effective, proportionate and dissuasive.
